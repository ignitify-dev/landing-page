What happens when you hit a limit

Going over a quota gives a 429 with the reason, the limit, and what you actually used — and upgrading fixes it. Using something your plan does not include gives a 400 at deploy or a 403 at run, naming the capability.

What you won’t get is a 429 because the host is busy. That case returns 503, or the run is simply queued and dispatched when a slot frees. A transient full host is never reported as a plan problem, so you’re never told to upgrade when all you needed was to wait.

Changing plans

Your plan is pinned at the version you were granted. Plans are never edited in place — a price or limit change publishes a new version, and existing customers stay on theirs. We move a whole plan only to correct an error, or with notice.

Grandfathering isn’t a policy anyone has to remember here; it’s how the tier system is built.
